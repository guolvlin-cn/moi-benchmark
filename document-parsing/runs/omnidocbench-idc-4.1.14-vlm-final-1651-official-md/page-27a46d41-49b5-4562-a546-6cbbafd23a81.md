Remark 2.2. We have used the version of the vector representation $\mathcal{V}_{q_1}^*$ that is dual to the representation conventionally used in the literature (see e.g. [4, 5]). In our convention this means that the operator $R(x|v)$ acts on a wavefunction that corresponds to a diagram sitting above the crossing in the picture.
    Using Theorem 2.1 it is easy to find the $R$-matrix acting on $(\mathcal{V}_{q_1}^*)^{\otimes N}\otimes \mathcal{F}_{q_2,q_3}^{(1,0)}(v)$: it is given by the composition of $N$ $R$-matrices (2.1) along the horizontal line. The result is a difference operator in $x_i$, $i = 1,\dots ,N$ which is also a vertex operator acting on $\mathcal{F}_{q_2,q_3}^{(1,0)}(v)$.
Definition 2.3. Let $\mathcal{O}(\vec{x}|v): (\mathcal{V}_{q_1}^*)^{\otimes N} \to (\mathcal{V}_{q_1}^*)^{\otimes N}$ be a matrix element of the R-matrix acting on $(\mathcal{V}_{q_1}^*)^{\otimes N} \otimes \mathcal{F}_{q_2,q_3}^{(1,0)}(v)$ between the vacuum states in the Fock representation:
  $$\begin{array}{l} \mathcal{O} _ {N} (\vec{x} | v) = \langle \emptyset , v | \check{\mathcal{R}} | _ {(\mathcal{V} _ {q _ {1}} ^ {*}) ^ {\otimes N} \otimes \mathcal{F} _ {q _ {2}, q _ {3}} ^ {(1, 0)} (v)} | \emptyset , v \rangle = \\ = \langle \varnothing , v | \prod_ {i = 1} ^ {\vec{N}} R _ {q _ {2}, q _ {3}} ^ {q _ {1}} \left(x _ {i} | v\right) | \varnothing , v \rangle = \quad \langle \varnothing , v | \longrightarrow \begin{array}{\text{ccccc} c} & & & \dots & & \\ & \downarrow & \downarrow & \downarrow & \downarrow & \downarrow \\ & \downarrow & \downarrow & \dots & \downarrow & \downarrow \end{array} \quad | \varnothing , v \rangle \tag{2.3} \\ \end{array}$$
where $x_{i}$ is understood as the variable on which the functions in the $i$-th $\mathcal{V}_{q_1}^*$ tensor factor depend. We call $\mathcal{O}(\vec{x}|v)$ the transfer matrix.
Definition 2.4. Trigonometric Ruijsenaars-Schneider Hamiltonians are the following difference operators

        $$H_{k}^{\mathrm{tRS},(N)}(\vec{x},q_{1},q_{2}) = q_{2}^{-\frac{k(k - 1)}{2}}\sum_{\substack{I\subseteq \{1,\ldots ,N\} \\ |I| = k}}\prod_{i\in I}\prod_{j\notin I}\frac{q_{2}^{-1}x_{i} - x_{j}}{x_{i} - x_{j}}\prod_{i\in I}q_{1}^{x_{i}\partial_{x_{i}}},\qquad k = 0,\ldots ,N. \tag{2.4}$$
    Trigonometric RS Hamiltonians commute [3] and their common polynomial eigenfunctions are given by Macdonald symmetric polynomials $P_{\lambda}(\vec{x})$:
          $$H _ {k} ^ {\mathrm{tRS}, (N)} (\vec{x}, q _ {1}, q _ {2}) P _ {\lambda} ^ {(q _ {1}, q _ {2})} (\vec{x}) = e _ {k} (\vec{u}) P _ {\lambda} ^ {(q _ {1}, q _ {2})} (\vec{x}), \tag{2.5}$$
where $u_{i} = q_{1}^{\lambda_{i}}q_{2}^{i - N}$ for $i = 1,\dots ,N$, $e_k$ are elementary symmetric functions and $\lambda = (\lambda_1,\lambda_2,\ldots ,\lambda_N)$ is a partition (Young diagram). In what follows we will mostly be interested in the eigenfunctions for generic parameters $u_{i}$; these are non-polynomial.
    We notice that $\mathcal{O}_N(\vec{x} |v)$ is the generating function of tRS Hamiltonians:
Theorem 2.5.

      $$(F _ {q _ {1}, q _ {2}} (\vec{x})) ^ {- 1} \mathcal{O} _ {N} (\vec{x} | v) F _ {q _ {1}, q _ {2}} (\vec{x}) = q _ {2} ^ {\frac{N (N - 1)}{2}} \sum_ {k = 0} ^ {N} (- v / q _ {1}) ^ {k - N} H _ {k} ^ {\mathrm{tRS}, (N)} (\vec{x}, q _ {1}, q _ {2}) q _ {1} ^ {- \frac{1}{2} \sum_ {i = 1} ^ {N} x _ {i} \partial_ {x _ {i}}}, \tag{2.6}$$
where

    $$F _ {q _ {1}, q _ {2}} (\vec{x}) = \prod_ {k <   l} ^ {N} \left[ x _ {k} ^ {- \frac{\ln q _ {2}}{\ln q _ {1}}} \frac{\left(\frac{x _ {l}}{x _ {k}} ; q _ {1}\right) _ {\infty}}{\left(q _ {2} ^ {- 1} \frac{x _ {l}}{x _ {k}} ; q _ {1}\right) _ {\infty}} \right]. \tag{2.7}$$
Remark 2.6. The overall shift $q_1^{-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}x_i\partial_{x_i}}$ commutes with all $H_k^{\mathrm{tRS},(N)}(\vec{x},q_1,q_2)$ .
