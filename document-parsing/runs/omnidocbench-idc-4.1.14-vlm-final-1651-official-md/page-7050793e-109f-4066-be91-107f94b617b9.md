      $$\begin{array}{l} \omega \left(a _ {2} a _ {3}, a _ {1}\right) + \omega \left(a _ {1} a _ {3}, a _ {2}\right) - \omega \left(a _ {3} a _ {1}, a _ {2}\right) - \omega \left(a _ {2} a _ {1}, a _ {3}\right) \\ = \langle r ^ {\sharp} (\xi_ {2}) r ^ {\sharp} (\xi_ {3}), \xi_ {1} \rangle + \langle r ^ {\sharp} (\xi_ {1}) r ^ {\sharp} (\xi_ {3}), \xi_ {2} \rangle - \langle r ^ {\sharp} (\xi_ {3}) r ^ {\sharp} (\xi_ {1}), \xi_ {2} \rangle - \langle r ^ {\sharp} (\xi_ {2}) r ^ {\sharp} (\xi_ {1}), \xi_ {3} \rangle \\ = \sum_ {i, j} \left( \begin{array}{c} \langle \xi_ {1}, x _ {i} x _ {j} \rangle \langle \xi_ {2}, y _ {i} \rangle \langle \xi_ {3}, y _ {j} \rangle + \langle \xi_ {1}, x _ {i} \rangle \langle \xi_ {2}, y _ {i} x _ {j} \rangle \langle \xi_ {3}, y _ {j} \rangle \\ - \langle \xi_ {1}, x _ {j} \rangle \langle \xi_ {2}, x _ {i} y _ {j} \rangle \langle \xi_ {3}, y _ {i} \rangle\right) - \langle \xi_ {1}, x _ {j} \rangle \langle \xi_ {2}, x _ {i} \rangle \langle \xi_ {3} \\ = \left\langle \xi_ {1} \otimes \xi_ {2} \otimes \xi_ {3}, r _ {1 2} r _ {1 3} + r _ {1 2} r _ {2 3} - r _ {2 3} r _ {1 2} - r _ {2 3} r _ {1 3} \right\rangle . \\ \end{array}$$
That is, $\omega(a_2a_3, a_1) + \omega(a_1a_3, a_2) - \omega(a_3a_1, a_2) - \omega(a_2a_1, a_3) = 0$ for any $a_1, a_2, a_3 \in A$ if and only if $\langle \xi_1 \otimes \xi_2 \otimes \xi_3, [[r, r]] \rangle = 0$ for any $\xi_1, \xi_2, \xi_3 \in A^*$, if and only if $[[r, r]] = 0$. The proof is finished.
  Next, we show that each relative Rota-Baxter operator induce an anti-Leibniz bialgebra.

Proposition 3.20. Let $(A,\cdot)$ be an anti-Leibniz algebra and $(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})$ be a bimodule over $(A,\cdot)$. Let $P:M\to A$ be a linear map which is identified as an element in $(A\ltimes M^{*})\otimes (A\ltimes M^{*})$ through $\mathrm{Hom}(M,A)\cong A\otimes M^{*}\subset (A\ltimes M^{*})\otimes (A\ltimes M^{*})$. Then $r\coloneqq P + \tau(P)$ is a symmetric solution of the aLYBE in semi-direct product anti-Leibniz algebra $A\ltimes M^{*}$ if and only if $P$ is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of $(A,\cdot)$ associated to $(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})$.
Proof. Let $\{e_1, e_2, \dots, e_n\}$ be a basis of $M$ and $\{f_1, f_2, \dots, f_n\}$ be its dual basis in $M^*$. Then $P = \sum_{i=1}^{n} P(e_i) \otimes f_i \in (A \rtimes M^*) \otimes (A \rtimes M^*)$, $r = P - \tau(P) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} (P(e_i) \otimes f_i + f_i \otimes P(e_i))$, and
    $$r _ {1 2} r _ {1 3} = \sum_ {i, j = 1} ^ {n} P (e _ {i}) P (e _ {j}) \otimes f _ {i} \otimes f _ {j} - \mathfrak{l} ^ {*} (P (e _ {i})) (f _ {j}) \otimes f _ {i} \otimes P (e _ {j}) - (\mathfrak{l} ^ {*} - \mathfrak{r} ^ {*}) (P (e _ {j})) (f _ {i}) \otimes P (e _ {i}) \otimes f _ {j},$$
    $$r _ {1 2} r _ {2 3} = \sum_ {i, j = 1} ^ {n} P (e _ {i}) \otimes (\mathfrak{l} ^ {*} - \mathfrak{r} ^ {*}) (P (e _ {j})) (f _ {i}) \otimes f _ {j} - f _ {i} \otimes P (e _ {i}) P (e _ {j}) \otimes f _ {j} + f _ {i} \otimes \mathfrak{l} ^ {*} (P (e _ {i})) (f _ {j}) \otimes P (e _ {j}),$$
    $$r _ {2 3} r _ {1 2} = \sum_ {i, j = 1} ^ {n} P (e _ {j}) \otimes \mathfrak{l} ^ {*} (P (e _ {i})) (f _ {j}) \otimes f _ {i} - f _ {j} \otimes P (e _ {i}) P (e _ {j}) \otimes f _ {i} + f _ {j} \otimes (\mathfrak{l} ^ {*} - \mathfrak{r} ^ {*}) (P (e _ {j})) (f _ {i}) \otimes P (e _ {i}),$$
    $$r _ {2 3} r _ {1 3} = \sum_ {i, j = 1} ^ {n} - f _ {j} \otimes P (e _ {i}) \otimes (\mathfrak{l} ^ {*} - \mathfrak{r} ^ {*}) (P (e _ {j})) (f _ {i}) - P (e _ {j}) \otimes f _ {i} \otimes \mathfrak{l} ^ {*} (P (e _ {i})) (f _ {j}) + f _ {i} \otimes f _ {j} \otimes P (e _ {i}) P (e _ {j}).$$
Note that $\mathrm{I}^{*}(P(e_{i}))(f_{j}) = \sum_{k = 1}f_{j}(\mathrm{I}(P(e_{i}))(e_{k}))f_{k}$ , we get
  $$\begin{array}{l} \sum_ {i, j = 1} ^ {n} P \left(e _ {j}\right) \otimes \mathfrak{l} ^ {*} \left(P \left(e _ {i}\right)\right) \left(f _ {j}\right) \otimes f _ {i} = \sum_ {i, j, k = 1} ^ {n} f _ {j} \left(\mathfrak{l} \left(P \left(e _ {i}\right)\right) \left(e _ {k}\right)\right) P \left(e _ {j}\right) \otimes f _ {k} \otimes f _ {i} \\ = \sum_ {i, j = 1} ^ {n} P \Big (\sum_ {k = 1} ^ {n} f _ {k} (\mathrm{I} (P (e _ {i})) (e _ {j})) e _ {k} \Big) \otimes f _ {j} \otimes f _ {i} = \sum_ {i, j = 1} ^ {n} P (\mathrm{I} (P (e _ {i})) (e _ {j})) \otimes f _ {j} \otimes f _ {i}. \\ \end{array}$$
Similarly, we also have $\sum_{i,j=1}^{n} f_j \otimes \mathfrak{l}^*(P(e_j))(f_i) \otimes P(e_i) = f_j \otimes f_i \otimes P(\mathfrak{l}(P(e_i))(e_j))$, $\sum_{i,j=1}^{n} P(e_i) \otimes \mathfrak{r}^*(P(e_j))(f_i) \otimes f_j = \sum_{i,j=1}^{n} P(\mathfrak{r}(P(e_j))(e_i)) \otimes f_i \otimes f_j$ and $\sum_{i,j=1}^{n} f_i \otimes \mathfrak{r}^*(P(e_i))(f_j) \otimes P(e_i) = f_i \otimes f_j \otimes P(\mathfrak{r}(P(e_j))(e_i))$. Thus
  $$\begin{array}{l} r _ {1 2} r _ {1 3} + r _ {1 2} r _ {2 3} - r _ {2 3} r _ {1 2} - r _ {2 3} r _ {1 3} = \sum_ {i, j = 1} ^ {n} \left(\left(P (e _ {i}) P (e _ {j}) - P (\mathfrak{r} (P (e _ {j})) (e _ {i}) - P (\mathfrak{l} (P (e _ {i})) (e _ {j}))\right) \otimes f _ {i} \otimes f _ {j}\right) \\ \left. + f _ {i} \otimes f _ {j} \otimes \big (P (\mathfrak{l} (P (e _ {i})) (e _ {j})) + P (\mathfrak{r} (P (e _ {j})) (e _ {i})) - P (e _ {i}) P (e _ {j}) \big)\right). \\ \end{array}$$
Thus, $r$ is a solution of the aLYBE in $A \ltimes M^{*}$ if and only if $P$ is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of $(A,\cdot)$ associated to $(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})$. 

  As a direct conclusion, we have

Corollary 3.21. Let $(A,\cdot)$ be an anti-Leibniz algebra and $(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})$ be a bimodule over $(A,\cdot)$. If $P:M\to A$ is a relative Rota-Baxter operator of $(A,\cdot)$ associated to $(M,\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{r})$, then there is an anti-Leibniz algebra $(A\ltimes M^{*},\Delta_{r})$, where $\Delta_r$ is given by Eq. (3.1) for $r = P + \tau(P)$.
