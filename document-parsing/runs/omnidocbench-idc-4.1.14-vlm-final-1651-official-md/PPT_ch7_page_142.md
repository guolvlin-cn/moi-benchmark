The transpose $\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{T}}$ of a matrix $\mathbf{A} = [a_{jk}]$ is $\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{T}} = [a_{kj}]$; rows become columns and conversely (Sec. 7.2). Here, $\mathbf{A}$ need not be square. If it is and $\mathbf{A} = \mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{T}}$, then $\mathbf{A}$ is called symmetric; if $\mathbf{A} = -\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{T}}$, it is called skew-symmetric.
For a product, $(\mathbf{AB})^{\mathrm{T}} = \mathbf{B}^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{A}^{\mathrm{T}}$ (Sec. 7.2).
