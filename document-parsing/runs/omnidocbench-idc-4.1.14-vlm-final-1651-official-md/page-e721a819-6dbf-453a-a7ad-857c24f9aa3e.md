## .1. Expansion in the $\mathfrak{q}$-aspect. Since $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$ are cuspidal automorphic representations, We note that $\Phi_1 = \varphi_1\varphi_2^{\mathfrak{q}}$ is a rapid-decay function which is invariant under the congruence group $\mathbf{K}_0(\mathfrak{u}\mathfrak{v}\mathfrak{q})$, we apply Plancherel formula (See [Zac20, Theorem 2.8]) to the well-defined inner product (5.2) in the space of forms of level $\mathfrak{mn}\mathfrak{q}$. We have the following decomposition of the considered period

$$\mathcal{P} _ {\mathfrak{q}} (\mathfrak{l}, \Phi , \Phi) = \mathcal{G} _ {\mathfrak{q}} (\mathfrak{l}, \Phi , \Phi) + \mathcal{C} _ {1}, \tag{5.3}$$
where the generic part is given by

    $$\mathcal{G}_{\mathfrak{q}}(\mathfrak{l},\Phi ,\Phi) = \sum_{\substack{\pi \text{cuspidal}\\ \mathrm{c}(\pi)|\mathfrak{u}\mathfrak{v}\mathfrak{q}}}\lambda_{\pi}(\mathfrak{l})\sum_{\psi \in \mathcal{B}(\pi ,\mathfrak{u}\mathfrak{v}\mathfrak{q})}\left|\left\langle \varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}^{\mathfrak{q}},\psi \right\rangle \right|^{2}$$
$$+ \sum_ {\substack{\chi \in F ^ {\times} \setminus \mathbb{A} _ {F} ^ {1} \\ c (\chi) ^ {2} | \mathfrak{u v q}}} \int_ {- \infty} ^ {\infty} \lambda_ {\chi , i t} (\mathfrak{l}) \sum_ {\psi_ {i t} \in \mathcal{B} \left(\chi , \chi^ {- 1}, i t, \mathfrak{u v q}\right)} \left| \left\langle \varphi_ {1} \varphi_ {2} ^ {\mathfrak{q}}, \mathrm{E} \left(\psi_ {i t}\right) \right\rangle \right| ^ {2} \frac{d t}{4 \pi}. \tag{5.4}$$
Moreover, the constant term $\mathcal{C}_1$ is the one-dimensional contribution (constant term) which appears only if both $\pi_1$ and $\pi_2$ are cuspidal and there exists finite many quadratic characters $\chi$ (depending on the number field $F$) of $F^{\times} \backslash \mathbb{A}_F^1$ such that $\pi_1 \simeq \pi_2 \otimes \chi$ ($\chi = 1$ if $\pi_1 = \pi_2$ for example). The cardinality of such quadratic character $\chi$ is $O_{\varepsilon,F}((uvq)^{\varepsilon}) = O_{\varepsilon,F}(q^{\varepsilon})$. For such a quadratic character $\chi$, by applying the Hecke relation (3.5), we have the following:
  $$\begin{array}{l} \mathcal{C} _ {1} = V _ {F} ^ {- 1} \cdot \langle \mathrm{T} _ {\mathfrak{l}} (\Phi), \varphi_ {\chi} \rangle \langle \varphi_ {\chi}, \Phi \rangle = \frac{\chi (\mathfrak{l}) \deg (\mathrm{T} _ {\mathfrak{l}})}{V _ {F}} \cdot \left| \left\langle \varphi_ {1} \varphi_ {2} ^ {\mathfrak{q}}, \varphi_ {\chi} \right\rangle \right| ^ {2} \\ = \frac{\zeta_ {\mathfrak{q}} (2) ^ {2} \chi (\mathfrak{l}) \deg \left(\mathrm{T} _ {\mathfrak{l}}\right)}{\zeta_ {\mathfrak{q}} (1) ^ {2} V _ {F}} \cdot \left| \frac{1}{q ^ {1 / 2}} \left(\left\langle \varphi_ {1} \cdot \left(\mathrm{T} _ {\mathfrak{q}} \varphi_ {2}\right), \varphi_ {\chi} \right\rangle - \frac{1}{q _ {1}} \cdot \left\langle \varphi_ {1} \cdot \left(\mathrm{T} _ {\mathfrak{q} _ {1} ^ {m - 2}} \varphi_ {2}\right), \varphi_ {\chi} \right\rangle\right) \right| ^ {2} \\ = \frac{\zeta_ {\mathfrak{q}} (2) ^ {2} \chi (\mathfrak{l}) \deg \left(\mathrm{T} _ {\mathfrak{l}}\right)}{\zeta_ {\mathfrak{q}} (1) ^ {2} V _ {F}} \cdot \left| \frac{1}{q ^ {1 / 2}} \left(\lambda_ {\pi_ {2}} (\mathfrak{q}) - \frac{1}{q _ {1}} \lambda_ {\pi_ {2}} \left(\mathfrak{q} _ {1} ^ {m - 2}\right)\right) \cdot \langle \varphi_ {1} \varphi_ {2}, \varphi_ {\chi} \rangle \right| ^ {2} \\ = \frac{\zeta_ {\mathfrak{q}} (2) ^ {2} \chi (\mathfrak{l}) \deg \left(\mathrm{T} _ {\mathfrak{l}}\right)}{q \zeta_ {\mathfrak{q}} (1) ^ {2} V _ {F}} \cdot \left| \left(\lambda_ {\pi_ {2}} (\mathfrak{q}) - \frac{1}{q _ {1}} \lambda_ {\pi_ {2}} \left(\mathfrak{q} _ {1} ^ {m - 2}\right)\right) \right| ^ {2} \cdot | \langle \varphi_ {1} \varphi_ {2}, \varphi_ {\chi} \rangle | ^ {2}, \\ \end{array}$$
where $\mathrm{T}_{\mathfrak{q}}$ is the Hecke operator and $\zeta_{\mathfrak{q}}(s) \coloneqq \prod_{v|\mathfrak{q}} \zeta_{F_v}(s)$ is the partial Dedekind zeta function. If the integral ideal $\mathfrak{q}$ is squarefree, i.e. $m = 1$, we define $\mathrm{T}_{\mathfrak{q}_1^{m-2}} \coloneqq 0$ and $\lambda_{\pi_2}(\mathfrak{q}_1^{m-2}) \coloneqq 0$. Hence, $\mathrm{T}_{\mathfrak{q}_1^{m-2}}\varphi_2 = 0$. Moreover, the degree of the Hecke operator $\mathrm{T}_{\mathfrak{l}}$ is defined by
$$\deg \left(\mathrm{T} _ {\mathrm{l}}\right) := \frac{1}{\ell^ {1 / 2}} \sum_ {0 \leqslant k \leqslant \frac{n}{2}} \gamma_ {n - 2 k} = \ell^ {1 / 2} \frac{\zeta_ {F _ {\mathfrak{p}}} (1)}{\zeta_ {F _ {\mathfrak{p}}} (n + 1)} \leqslant \ell^ {1 / 2} \zeta_ {F _ {\mathfrak{p}}} (1). \tag{5.6}$$
Since $\pi_2$ is $\theta_{2}$-tempered at all the finite places, we have $|\lambda_{\pi_2}(\mathfrak{q})| \leqslant (m + 1) \cdot q^{\theta_2}$. Applying Cauchy-Schwartz inequality,
      $$\mathcal{C} _ {1} \ll_ {\pi_ {1}, \pi_ {2}, F, \varepsilon} (\ell q) ^ {\varepsilon} \frac{\ell^ {1 / 2}}{q ^ {1 - 2 \theta_ {2}}} \ll_ {\pi_ {1}, \pi_ {2}, F, \varepsilon} (\ell q) ^ {\varepsilon} \frac{\ell^ {1 / 2}}{q ^ {2 5 / 3 2}}$$
since $0 \leqslant \theta_{2} \leqslant \frac{7}{64}$. Hence we can conclude
$$\mathcal{P} _ {\mathfrak{q}} (\mathfrak{l}, \Phi , \Phi) = \mathcal{G} _ {\mathfrak{q}} (\mathfrak{l}, \Phi , \Phi) + O _ {\pi_ {1}, \pi_ {2}, F, \varepsilon} \left(\left(\ell q\right) ^ {\varepsilon} \frac{\ell^ {1 / 2}}{q ^ {1 - 2 \theta_ {2}}}\right), \tag{5.7}$$
where we recall that $\Phi$ is defined as (5.1).
